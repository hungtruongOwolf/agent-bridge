# Video brief: who watches, what they want, what we say

There is no marketing skill installed here, so this applies the usual rules by hand: know the listener, lead with their problem,
show proof, say one thing they will repeat, and be honest where honesty earns trust.

## Who is listening

Judges who watch dozens of entries in one sitting, mostly muted at first, deciding in the first ten seconds whether to pay attention.

| Hackathon | What the judges score | What they want to feel by the end |
|---|---|---|
| Nebius x NVIDIA | Technological implementation, design, potential impact, quality of the idea (equal weight) | "This is a clever idea, it really runs on Nemotron, and I could imagine using it." |
| PayPal AI | Technological implementation, design, potential impact, innovation, presentation (works end to end, clear problem, who, why) | "Agents really paid agents here, with a thought-through answer to what goes wrong." |

(From our own research notes; the exact prize wording was not published, so the PayPal prize names are inferred from these criteria.)

## The one line

**Rent a stronger agent. Keep your data.** (PayPal cut: **Agents that pay agents, on evidence.**) Said at the start of the idea, and again as the last words.

## The shape (the same in both, because it works)

1. **Pain in one breath (about 10 seconds).** A trade nobody wants to make. No product name yet. Plain words, a picture that needs no explaining.
2. **The turn.** One sentence that flips the problem: the agent comes to your data, not your data to the agent.
3. **Proof by showing, in the real product.** Short scenes, one idea each, always something moving. Never a slide of bullet points.
4. **The scary question, answered live.** "What if the helper is lying?" Show it being caught. This is the moment judges remember.
5. **Why it matters.** Who benefits (people with private notes, specialists who want to be paid), said in one breath.
6. **Honest close.** One real limit, said plainly. Then the line again, the licence, the link.

## Rules for the words

- Short sentences. One idea each. Read aloud, no breath problems.
- Plain English: "the little office", "a sealed room", "shelves", not broker, sidecar, digest, ledger.
- Numbers only when they are real and on screen (forty cents, a hundred and fifty notes).
- The product name appears after the pain, never before it.
- Say what is real. Where a scene is a practice server, a recording or test money, the caption says so.

## What we do not say

"Nothing can ever leak." "Certified means safe." "Real money." "Live PayPal" (until the sandbox is checked end to end).
Anything about Hosted mode (designed, not built). The old improvement numbers (0.39 to 0.55): they come from an earlier suite and pack versions.

## What each video is for

- **Nebius:** Idea and design. Sealed room, shelves, a caught liar, consent, a marketplace that ranks on evidence. Nemotron on Nebius is named once, on screen
  in the caption and out loud; Tavily is named once in the close.
- **PayPal:** Agentic commerce. Hold, floor fee, accept or not, five ways to charge, evidence decides disputes, tested for repeats, races and crashes.
  Everything is test money, and the caption on the money scenes says which provider is on screen.
